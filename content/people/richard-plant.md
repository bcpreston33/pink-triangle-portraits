Richard Plant (Plaut) was a gay Jewish writer who was instrumental in getting information about this history to the English speaking world. He wrote *The Pink Triangle: The Nazi War Against Homosexuals* in 1986 and offered audiences an expanded view of a history that was repeatedly denied and hidden. 

Plant grew up in Frankfurt, Germany, and knew he was gay and had relationships with other boys starting when he was 16 in his youth group. His godfather was a modern therapist and helped his parents accept Richard’s orientation. In college he enjoyed acting and studying, and met the author Klaus Mann who had written openly queer literature. As the Nazis came to power, Richard fled to Switzerland to continue his studies and work as a writer. While in Basel, he was able to hear about what was happening in Germany, as their newspapers were not as censored. Richard stayed in contact with his many gay friends still back in Germany. Many were trying to escape, but did not have the resources or ability to do so. 

With immense help, Richard was able to escape to America in 1938. In NYC he worked as an editorial assistant for an anti-fascist publication and as a scriptwriter and translator for NBC’s war coverage. During the war he met many queer US servicemen. After the war Richard wrote novels and taught German. In the 50’s he wrote for *Der Kreis*, penning happy stories about gay life in Massachusetts and New York, and queer love stories that were also anti-racist as well.

**After retiring as a professor, Plant began real work on his Pink Triangle book. He felt as someone that had escaped he had a duty to tell this history to the world.** He was very concerned that popular histories of the war had ignored queer people, and worse, attributed queerness to nazism itself. As someone who knew people from Magnus Hirschfeld to Alfred Kinsey, Richard had a great insight into the gay rights movement and its history. He took many trips back to Germany to complete his research, and published his book in 1986.
