For this project, I am working with three main sources of photographs: Personal photos from victims' lives (when available), arrest mugshots, and concentration camp intake photos.

In my work, I aim to bring these victims into clarity for a modern audience, adding color to the black-and-white originals to remind us that these were real people who you could imagine passing on the street today. We know he was a waiter, but what was Kurt like as a real, living breathing queer man? Here he lives as his true self, beautiful inside and out. I hope Kurt felt such joy in his life. I hope that this project presents not just information on queer individuals from this time period, but possibility. Possibilities of what was and could have been and could still be.

Kurt Bruhn was born in Königsberg, Bavaria, on December 20, 1913. He worked as a waiter before being arrested and sent to Auschwitz on November 27, 1941. His prisoner number, 23520, was tattooed onto him.

He survived 11 months before dying at just 28 years old. However, my research uncovered conflicting reports, one stating he perished in Auschwitz, another indicating he was transferred to Mauthausen two months later and may have died there instead.
